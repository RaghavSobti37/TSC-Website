Culture Essay

From Bhajan to Clubbing, From Mythology to Cinema

How Indian culture is finding new mainstream forms across music, screens, gatherings, and everyday identity.

Rohit Sobti Jul 27, 2026 11 min read on Medium

This local version presents the essay as a TSC editorial preview. The full article remains on Medium.

Read on Medium

The Core Idea

The essay argues that Indian culture is not being newly discovered. It is being re-staged, remixed, and re-entered through formats that contemporary audiences already understand: nightlife, cinema, streaming, festivals, social media, and community rituals.

"Indian culture is not suddenly becoming relevant."

That distinction matters. Culture does not become powerful only when it becomes fashionable. It becomes visible when the formats around it make space for its meaning, rhythm, memory, and emotional force.

Why It Matters

For artists and cultural builders, the opportunity is not to dilute tradition for scale. The opportunity is to build new containers for old intelligence. A bhajan can sit beside bass. Mythology can move through cinema. Devotion, identity, sound, and story can travel into rooms that once seemed disconnected from them.

  • Rooted ideas can reach mass audiences when they are designed with present-day behavior in mind.
  • Popular culture works best when it creates belonging, not just spectacle.
  • Indian stories have the depth to cross formats without losing their emotional center.

TSC Lens

This is close to The Shakti Collective's larger belief: sustainable creative work grows from identity. Artists do not need to choose between heritage and relevance. The stronger path is to understand the source deeply, then shape it with craft, courage, and contemporary fluency.
